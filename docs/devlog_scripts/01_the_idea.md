# Devlog 01 — The Game I Couldn't Find

**Series:** Prokaryon devlogs
**Runtime:** about 5:00 to 5:30 (about 800 spoken words)
**Form:** voiceover over other games' footage, plus a few visuals made for this video
**Tone:** casual, first person, like you're telling a friend why you started. Fond of the other games. Specific when you criticize them.

Read it out loud once and cut whatever makes you stumble. Lines in *[brackets]* are direction, not words to say. If a personal beat isn't true, swap it for the true one. The argument still works.

## Script

### Cold open — 0:00

*[Montage of the five games. No sting.]*

If you're watching this, you've probably already played one of these. Spore. The Bibites. Thrive. Cell Lab. Sipho. I like them. I still haven't found the game I want. So I'm making it. It's called Prokaryon, and this video is just the idea.

### Spore — 0:15

*[Stay on the cell stage.]*

I've had this in my head for a long time, and Spore is what gave it a shape. Start as a cell, end as a galaxy. I thought the cell was the real fantasy. Design a tiny body, give it a way to move, drop it somewhere that doesn't care about you, and see if the idea survives.

Then you play it. Green dots or red dots. A mouth. A spike. An arcade game that borrowed a microscope. The moment you wonder what a life like that would actually be, the game takes the microscope away.

*[Creature creator, just long enough to smile.]*

The creature creator is still incredible. You build a body and you believe it lives somewhere. I wanted that aimed at a cell, and I wanted it to last. Not a stage. A lineage.

### The rest of the shelf — 1:10

*[Bibites, untouched. Then Thrive with a hand on the keys. Then Cell Lab. Then Sipho. About fifteen seconds each.]*

Every few years something else looks like it might be the one.

The Bibites is the simulation I trust. Leave it running and you come back to a population you didn't design. You're still outside the glass. I want to be the lineage.

Thrive got the closest to a grown-up cell stage. An editor, real compounds, biology that tries. Then you hold W and fly the cell. The water changes, and your hands answer, not anything you built.

Cell Lab lets you program a colony to solve a puzzle, and when it clicks, it really clicks. The other cells are still pieces of your solution. Nobody else is out there with a species and plans of their own.

Sipho lets you build a beautiful little animal and drive it. A flagellum is a thruster. A spike is a weapon. It never asks what that motor costs the cell.

### The argument — 2:15

*[Your graphic. A real mechanism, then the icon that replaced it.]*

Same move every time. A real idea gets sanded down into a verb you already know. Eat. Steer. Bolt on a part. The bet is that the realistic version would feel like homework, so the game should protect you from it.

I don't think that respects the player.

A gene that only turns on in salt water. A flagellum built on the dark side of the cell. A reserve that saves you and makes you too bulky to swim. That's the game, if you can see the cause and the cost is real.

### Pseudomonas — 2:50

*[Two cells. A needle. Dots cross. The recipient changes what it's doing. It does not flash red.]*

Pseudomonas syringae lives on plants. It grows a pilus, a hollow needle, part of a type three secretion system, and it injects effector proteins through that needle into the plant cell. They don't just do damage. They shut the immune response down and push the plant's metabolism around until the bacterium can stay.

One organism, editing another from the inside. I don't want that as a boss cooldown. I want a player to evolve it, and another player's species to notice it, block it, or steal the gene.

### The vision — 3:25

*[Your water. The species name stays on screen through the death.]*

So. Prokaryon.

You play a species, and you name it. A cell can die. You continue as whatever's left. The life ends when the population is gone.

You don't steer. No WASD, and clicking the water doesn't move you. If your cells swim, it's because you built a flagellum, a motor, a sense for light or sugar or salt, and a rule for when they turn on. Put the motor on one end. Leave it off until things get bad. Then you watch. When it fails, you change the genome. Sounds like an idle game. The play is figuring out why you died.

The water has weather. Heat, light, acid, salt, current, and quiet pockets where the chemistry sits still. You afford the next gene by reproducing. Cells grow, cells divide, division pays for the attempt. A bigger cell stores more and costs more. A gene left on all the time spends what something else needed. Good ideas should be able to wreck you.

*[Two colors of cell. A leak, then a needle, then a gene jumping across.]*

Other people are in the same water, with species they named.

They can feed you by accident, because their waste is your food. They can crowd you off a nutrient. They can grow the needle, touch your cell, and inject something that makes it stop obeying you. Or they pass you a gene. That's the kind of sex bacteria actually have: a trick jumps into your lineage. Sometimes it saves you. Sometimes the gift is what kills you, and you notice late.

No ally button. Contact, chemistry, and genes. You should be able to point at the screen and say why. Out of the current, behind the rock. Glowing, because the light gene is on. Dying, because somebody else reached in and changed their behavior.

*[Hold on the water. End card under the last line. Don't tour the prototype.]*

That's the game I've been failing to find. A microscope, a genome, and a sea full of other people's ideas.

## Visuals to make later

Keep these diagrammatic and a little handmade. They should feel like your video, not a textbook slide.

1. **The sanding-down graphic** (2:15). Three beats, same layout. Flagellum becomes a thruster arrow. Salt stress becomes a blue bar. A regulated gene becomes a checkbox labeled ON.
2. **The needle** (2:50). Two cells, a thin pilus, effector dots crossing. The recipient's "swim toward light" arrow flips. The attack is a rewrite.
3. **The lineage hop** (3:25). One cell fades. The view slides to a sister of the same color. The species name never leaves.
4. **The shared water** (4:15). Two colors. One leaks a trail the other feeds on. Then a pilus fires. Then a gene-tag jumps across on contact.
5. **End card.** The name Prokaryon, and "Devlog 01." The last sentence can finish over it.

## If the read runs long

Protect the vision. Trim, in order: the creature-creator compliment, the Sipho motor-cost sentence, then "Sounds like an idle game." Keep the needle, and keep the gene that jumps between players.

## Facts worth not flubbing

- *Pseudomonas syringae* is a plant pathogen. The needle is its type III secretion system. The tube outside the cell is the Hrp pilus. Effectors go into plant cells, where they suppress immunity and retune metabolism. Say "plant cell." The player-versus-player version is your extrapolation, and the script already frames it that way.
- Spore's cell stage is short, and it does sort you by mouth before it moves on. The creature-creator line is what keeps this from sounding like a grudge.
- Thrive does let you pilot the cell. Show hands on the keys if the footage allows. That steering is the contrast.
- Cell Lab and Sipho are doing different jobs on purpose. The line is that they aren't this game.
