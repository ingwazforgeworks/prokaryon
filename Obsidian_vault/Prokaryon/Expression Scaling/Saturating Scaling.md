\(F(E)=\dfrac{aE}{K+E}\)

Output rises steeply at low abundance and approaches the ceiling \(a\) asymptotically. \(K\) is the abundance giving half-maximum output.

**Used by:** [[Carbex Permease (CBXP)]], [[Oxidex Reductase (OXDR)]], [[Sulfex Reductase (SLFR)]], [[Photorhodin (PHOR)]], [[Flagellar Motor Protein (FLGM)]], receptors

**Why it is the most common choice.** Saturation is what makes over-expression pointless rather than merely expensive, so a player who doubles a gene's level and sees almost no change has learned something true about the cell. It also caps the payoff of any single gene without needing a hand-placed limit.

For genes limited by something outside the cell — contact area, local acceptor supply, light intensity — the ceiling \(a\) should be set by that external quantity rather than by the gene.
