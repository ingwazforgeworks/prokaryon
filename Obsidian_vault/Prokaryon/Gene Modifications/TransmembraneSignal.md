**Modification Name:** Transmembrane Signal  
**Type:** [[Gene Modification]] — localization  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Destination:** [[Plasma Membrane]] — the protein is anchored in the envelope, active side facing out  
**Applies to:** Hydrolases, receptors, adhesins

A tag that embeds a protein in the membrane instead of releasing it. The active site still faces the water, but the protein stays with the cell.

### What anchoring buys

**Products land where they can be imported.** An anchored [[Glycon Hydrolase (GLYH)]] releases [[Carbohydron]] a membrane's thickness from the permease that takes it up, so the cell captures most of its own work. A secreted one donates a large share to the neighbourhood.

**The enzyme is not spent.** Anchored proteins persist; secreted ones are gone the moment they drift away. Over a cell's lifetime that is the difference between paying once and paying continuously.

**Contact becomes a strategy.** A cell that can only digest what it is touching has a reason to stick to surfaces — which is what makes [[Adhesin (ADHN)]] and [[Surfaces and Geometry]] matter rather than being decoration. Anchored digestion plus adhesion is the debris-scavenger strategy in the [[Metabolic Map]].

### What anchoring costs

Reach. Substrate that never contacts the envelope is invisible, so an anchored lineage in open water with dispersed food starves next to a secreting one. The trade is laid out in full in [[SecretoryPeptide]].

### On receptors

Receptors are anchored by default and the tag is usually redundant on them, but it is not always: a receptor for an internal pool belongs in the [[Cytosol]], and one sensing the water belongs here. Making that explicit per gene is better than a hidden rule, and it keeps receptor-gated sensing honest — the cell knows what its receptors are pointed at, and so does the player looking at the [[Personal Cell Map]].
