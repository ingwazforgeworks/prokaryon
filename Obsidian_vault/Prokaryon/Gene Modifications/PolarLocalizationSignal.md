**Modification Name:** Polar Localization Signal  
**Type:** [[Gene Modification]] — localization  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Destination:** One end of the cell rather than distributed around it  
**Applies to:** [[Flagellin (FLGN)]], [[Cilium Assemblase (CILA)]], [[Adhesin (ADHN)]], [[Cohesin (COHS)]], receptors, [[Pilin (PILN)]]

A tag that concentrates a protein at a pole instead of letting it appear anywhere on the envelope.

### Why it matters for motility

Flagella scattered around a cell push in opposing directions and largely cancel. Gathered at one pole they sum, and the cell swims. So this tag is what turns [[Flagellin (FLGN)]] and [[Flagellar Motor Protein (FLGM)]] from a metabolic cost into locomotion.

Real cells maintain polar placement with dedicated machinery — source [B1] — and the [[Gene Catalog]] originally modelled that as a *Flagellar Positioner* gene. As a modification the arrangement is better: positioning is a property of how the flagellum is built, not a separate organ, and the player reads it as one decision about one gene rather than a mandatory second purchase.

### Why it matters beyond motility

**It makes [[Photovector (PHVC)]] work.** Directional light response needs a receptor whose signal differs across the cell. A polar receptor supplies that asymmetry; a uniformly distributed one cannot, no matter how sensitive. See [[Photovector (PHVC)]] and source [B2].

**It makes adhesion selective.** Polar [[Adhesin (ADHN)]] sticks a cell to a surface by one end, leaving the rest exposed to the water — the difference between anchoring and being buried. Polar [[Cohesin (COHS)]] grips same-type neighbours by one end, so the cell joins a clump without being buried in it.

**It makes contact-dependent genes aimable.** [[Pilin (PILN)]] and anything downstream of it act on whatever the pole is against, which gives direction to [[Horizontal Gene Transfer]] and to contact-based antagonism.

### What it costs

Polar placement gives up coverage. A polar receptor samples one direction, a polar adhesin grips one spot, and a polar cohesin holds fewer neighbours, so the gain in directionality is paid for in how much of the world the cell can feel or hold at once.

### Open question

Whether the player picks *which* pole, or only "a pole", matters. Choosing both poles independently edges toward the direct steering that confirmed requirement V02 forbids; choosing "polar" and letting the cell sort out the geometry stays on the right side of it. The conservative reading is the second one.
