**Resource Name:** Phycin  
**Type:** Pigment — cell-internal  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Absorbs:** Green and orange light — the band [[Chlorin]] leaves behind  
**Description:** A protein-bound open-chain pigment forming a large accessory antenna. Bulky and nitrogen-hungry, and the only pigment that thrives specifically in light other phototrophs have already used.  
**Source:** [[Phycin Synthase (PHYS)]]  
**Uses:** Accessory antenna feeding [[Reaction Center (RXNC)]]; enables a green- or orange-sensing [[Photochrome (PCHR)]].

**A niche made entirely by other players.** Phycin is worthless in fresh, full-spectrum light, where [[Chlorin]] outperforms it for less material. It becomes correct only beneath or beside an established [[Chlorin]] population that has stripped the red and blue out — so this pigment's habitat does not exist until somebody else creates it.

That is the clearest case in the [[Gene Catalog]] of a strategy whose viability is determined by the ecology rather than by the environment, and it is the kind of outcome specification §7.4 asks the emergence gate to demonstrate.

**Very expensive in nitrogen.** Phycin is a large protein-pigment assembly, so it competes hard with [[Anabolase (ANAB)]] for [[Nitrox]]. A Phycin lineage in nitrogen-poor water is paying the highest material cost in [[Phototrophy]] for the poorest light available — which should be survivable and never dominant.
