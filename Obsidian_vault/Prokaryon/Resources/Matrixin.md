**Resource Name:** Matrixin  
**Type:** Secreted structural polymer  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Description:** A sticky extracellular polymer that accumulates around dense groups of cells, forming a biofilm. Unlike other secretions it does not dissolve and drift away: it stays where it is made, which is what makes it useful and what makes it occupiable.  
**Source:** Secreted by [[Matrixin Synthase (MTXS)]], at a substantial carbon cost.  
**Uses:** Slows local [[Flow]], retains dissolved nutrients, impedes diffusion of [[Lysin]] inward, and provides attachment substrate.

**It builds a niche.** Matrixin is the main way players can alter their own environment rather than adapt to it. A mature biofilm has measurably different transport properties from the water around it: nutrients released by hydrolases stay put longer, toxins penetrate slower, and current no longer strips cells away. Specification §7.2 lists low flow, nutrient trapping, and protected surfaces as the levers a microniche uses, and a biofilm pulls all three.

**It also traps waste.** The same impeded transport that keeps nutrients in keeps [[Fermentate]] and acidity in. A biofilm interior drifts toward low [[Oxidex]] and low pH as it thickens, so the deep inside of a successful colony becomes a different, harsher habitat than its surface. That gradient is generated by the players, not authored.

**Squatters.** Anything inside the matrix benefits from it, including cells that contributed nothing and cells of other species. Compare [[Capsulin]], which protects only its producer — the pair exists so the cost of cooperating is visible next to the cost of not cooperating.
