**Resource Name:** Osmolyn  
**Type:** Cell-internal solute  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Description:** A small compatible solute the cell accumulates to match the osmotic pressure outside it. It participates in nothing else: its only job is to be present in the right concentration so that water stops moving in the wrong direction.  
**Source:** Synthesised by [[Osmolyn Synthase (OSMS)]] from carbon and [[ATP]].  
**Uses:** Balances internal against external water potential in high or variable [[Salinity]].

**Expensive in carbon, not energy.** The cost of osmotic tolerance is mostly material: a cell in saline water spends a meaningful share of its carbon on molecules that will never become [[Biomass]]. That is why salinity acts as a growth tax rather than a barrier, and why a salt specialist grows slowly even when fed.

**Slow, and that is the point.** Building Osmolyn takes time, so it defends against a sustained salinity difference and not against a sudden one. [[Aquaporin (AQUP)]] is the fast counterpart, controlling water flux immediately but buffering nothing. A cell in a habitat with a stable gradient wants Osmolyn; a cell crossing between zones wants both, and pays twice.

**Open:** specification §7.1 asks whether osmotic adaptation is reversible expression or long-term specialisation. Osmolyn as written is reversible, which keeps regulation meaningful. The alternative — a permanent envelope specialisation — would move this content into [[Morphology]] and remove the promoter decision entirely.
