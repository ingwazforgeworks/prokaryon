**Resource Name:** Reducin  
**Type:** Cell-internal intermediate — reducing power  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Description:** The cell's supply of chemical reducing power: the electrons needed to build reduced molecules rather than merely to drive reactions. Distinct from [[ATP]], which pays for work, and from [[Fluxin]], which carries energy between systems.  
**Source:** Produced from [[Fluxin]] by [[Reducin Dehydrogenase (RDCD)]], or directly alongside [[Fluxin]] by [[Reaction Center (RXNC)]].  
**Uses:** Consumed by [[Glycon Synthase (GLYS)]] to fix [[Carbex]] into [[Glycon]].

**Why this resource exists.** Specification §2.4 requires that carbon fixation need reducing equivalents in addition to energy, and that capturing light not be treated as equivalent to making biomass. Reducin is that requirement made concrete: it is the reason [[Photorhodin (PHOR)]] cannot lead to autotrophy on its own, and the reason the fixation chain is the longest dependency in the [[Gene Catalog]].

Reducin also creates a real internal allocation problem. Every unit diverted from [[Fluxin]] into Reducin is a unit not converted into [[ATP]], so an autotroph is permanently poorer in usable energy than a heterotroph burning the same amount of fuel.
