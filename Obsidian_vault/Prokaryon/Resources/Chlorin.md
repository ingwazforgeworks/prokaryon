**Resource Name:** Chlorin  
**Type:** Pigment — cell-internal  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Absorbs:** Red and blue light  
**Description:** The primary light-harvesting pigment: a ring structure with a central metal ion, expensive to build and by far the most efficient at delivering energy into a reaction centre.  
**Source:** [[Chlorin Synthase (CHLS)]]  
**Uses:** Required by [[Reaction Center (RXNC)]]; enables a red- or blue-sensing [[Photochrome (PCHR)]].

**The pigment that makes autotrophy possible.** No other pigment can feed a reaction centre well enough to produce [[Reducin]] at a useful rate, so Chlorin is a hard prerequisite on the path to fixing [[Carbex]]. See [[Metabolic Map]].

**It creates the shade it suffers from.** Chlorin strips red and blue out of the water above, so a dense Chlorin population leaves the light beneath it depleted at exactly the wavelengths a second Chlorin lineage would need. That is the mechanism behind spectral layering: [[Phycin]] and [[Rhodin]] lineages live in light that Chlorin cells have already filtered, and specification §7.4's emergence gate asks for exactly this kind of outcome arising from ordinary rules.

**Expensive in nitrogen.** The ring structure is nitrogen-rich, which puts Chlorin in direct competition with [[Anabolase (ANAB)]] for [[Nitrox]]. A phototroph in nitrogen-poor water cannot afford much antenna, which is a genuine constraint rather than a tuned cost.
