**Type note:** Insoluble Resource  
**Status:** Proposed

A resource that cannot diffuse, cannot be carried by [[Flow]], and cannot cross a membrane. It stays where it is until a cell spends energy on an extracellular enzyme to break it open — and the product of that work appears *outside* the cell.

### Why the category exists

Insoluble stocks are the engine of the game's social problems. Specification §7.4 asks what makes public-goods exploitation interesting, and this is the answer in material form:

1. A cell pays a gene, expression capacity, and upkeep for a hydrolase or lyase.
2. The enzyme releases a soluble nutrient into shared water.
3. Any neighbour with the matching permease eats it at no cost.

The producer's only defences are keeping the product close with [[TransmembraneSignal]] and keeping its neighbours the same type with [[Cohesin (COHS)]].

### The insoluble stocks

| Stock | Releases | Via | Renewing? |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Carbohydron]] | [[Glycon]] | [[Glycon Hydrolase (GLYH)]] | Yes, from death |
| [[Cerumen]] | [[Lipron]] | [[Lipron Hydrolase (LIPH)]] | Yes, from death |
| [[Azoite]] | [[Nitrox]] | [[Azoite Hydrolase (AZOH)]] | Slowly, from sediment |
| [[Thionite]] | [[Sulfex]] | [[Thionite Lyase (THNL)]] | No — finite per location |
| [[Ferracite]] | [[Ferron]] | [[Ferracite Reductase (FCTR)]] | Effectively unlimited |

[[Granulin]] is a special case: an insoluble store the cell builds *inside* itself, which nobody else can reach until the cell dies.
