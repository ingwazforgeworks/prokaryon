**Status:** Proposed — a strategy, not a gene category. The genes live in [[Metabolism]].

Turning [[Light]] into usable energy. Split into two independent branches on purpose: specification §2.1 warns against presenting rhodopsin-based phototrophy and reaction-centre phototrophy as one settled linear lineage.

| Branch | Genes | Gives | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pump | [[Rhodin Synthase (RHDS)]] → [[Photorhodin (PHOR)]] | [[Fluxin]] only | Cheap, two genes, permanent ceiling |
| Reaction centre | [[Chlorin Synthase (CHLS)]] → [[Reaction Center (RXNC)]] | [[Fluxin]] + [[Reducin]] | Nitrogen-hungry pigment, photodamage, upkeep |

The two share no progression edge. A pump lineage does not "upgrade" into a reaction-centre lineage; it starts the second branch from scratch.

### Light is energy, not food

Neither branch supplies carbon. A phototroph must still import organic carbon or complete the fixation chain through [[Carbex Permease (CBXP)]] and [[Glycon Synthase (GLYS)]] — and fixation needs [[Reducin]], which only the reaction-centre branch produces.

That is why [[Photorhodin (PHOR)]] is a subsidy rather than a living: it buys energy and no material. Specification §2.4 requires carbon fixation to demand reducing equivalents separately from [[ATP]], and this is where that requirement bites.

### Pigments decide which light you get

Four pigments, four bands. This is the substance of the strategy, because the spectrum is not uniform and does not stay uniform:

| Pigment | Band | Role |
|---|---|---|
| [[Rhodin]] | Mid-spectrum | Cheap; drives the pump directly |
| [[Chlorin]] | Red and blue | Primary antenna; the only route to [[Reducin]] |
| [[Carotin]] | Blue-violet | Accessory antenna and photoprotection |
| [[Phycin]] | Green and orange | Accessory antenna for filtered light |

Pigments are also the prerequisite for *sensing* light at all — see [[Photochrome (PCHR)]]. A cell can only detect the bands it has pigment for, so perception and harvest are bought from the same shelf.

### Spectral competition is the interesting part

A [[Chlorin]] population strips red and blue from the water beneath it. What is left is green and orange — useless to another Chlorin lineage, and exactly what [[Phycin]] harvests.

So depth layering in a lit habitat is not an environmental feature. It is produced by the phototrophs themselves, and a [[Phycin]] lineage's entire niche is created by its competitor. Specification §7.4's emergence gate asks for outcomes like this one arising from ordinary rules rather than from a rule about layering.

### Emission is the reverse

Pigments absorb [[Light]] and colour the body. [[Red Fluorescent Protein (RFP)]], [[Green Fluorescent Protein (GFP)]], [[Yellow Fluorescent Protein (YFP)]] and [[Blue Fluorescent Protein (BFP)]] spend [[ATP]] to emit one band. The glow is real light: a nearby phototroph can harvest it, and a [[Photochrome (PCHR)]] can read it, only when that cell already has a pigment covering the band.

| Gene | Band | Who can take it | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Red Fluorescent Protein (RFP)]] | Red | [[Chlorin]] | Cheapest emission |
| [[Green Fluorescent Protein (GFP)]] | Green | [[Phycin]] | Blends into a Chlorin shadow |
| [[Yellow Fluorescent Protein (YFP)]] | Yellow | [[Rhodin]] | Seen by the cheapest sensor |
| [[Blue Fluorescent Protein (BFP)]] | Blue | [[Chlorin]], [[Carotin]] | Dearest emission |

In sunlit water the environment swamps all four. They matter in the dark and at short range, which is also where emitting is an advertisement.

### The cost of living in the light

Exposure. Lit water is the best place for [[Oxidex]] respiration and the worst place to hide from [[Lysin]] and from predators, and excess light damages the cell unless [[Photoprotectin (PHPR)]] is expressed — which needs [[Carotin]], which is another gene.

A surface phototroph therefore carries at least four genes before it is competitive, while a heterotroph in the same water carries two. Whether that is the right ratio is a balance question the vault cannot answer.
