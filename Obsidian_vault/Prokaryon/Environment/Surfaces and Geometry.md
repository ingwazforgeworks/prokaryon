The solid world: rock, sediment, debris and the shapes they make. Geometry is not scenery, because three things in the catalog only work on contact and two fuels exist only in mineral form.

## Why surfaces are load-bearing

Specification §7.1 asks what differs between open water and a cave, and §2.4 requires local geometry to alter transport, exposure or retention if caves are to be meaningful. Surfaces earn that by being the only place several strategies can operate at all:

| Requires contact | Why |
|---|---|
| [[Thionite Lyase (THNL)]] | Dissolves mineral crust it is touching |
| [[Ferracite Reductase (FCTR)]] | Reduces mineral iron in place |
| [[TransmembraneSignal]] | Tethered enzymes reach only adjacent material |
| [[Pilin (PILN)]] | Pulls against a substrate |

A lineage built on any of these must solve attachment before it can eat, which pulls in [[Adhesin (ADHN)]] and forfeits mobility. That is a genuine strategic commitment produced entirely by geometry.

## Material tags

Surfaces are not interchangeable. A mask alone would make every wall the same wall.

| Tag | Carries | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Ferracite | [[Ferracite]] — unlimited | Permanent food, contested |
| Thionite | [[Thionite]] — finite | Rich then exhausted |
| Sediment | [[Azoite]], [[Sulfex]] | Nitrogen and anoxic respiration |
| Inert rock | Nothing | Attachment and shelter only |

## Geometry changes transport

Enclosure is what produces [[Redox Stratification]]. A restricted entrance slows [[Flow]], which starves the interior of [[Oxidex]] while retaining [[Fermentate]] and dissolved nutrients — so the same fuel supports a different metabolism inside than outside. Specification §7.2 lists exactly these levers and notes a microcave need not use all of them at once.

## The rule that constrains all of it

Specification §7.2 is explicit: success must not depend on precise manual movement the player is forbidden to perform. Every niche needs a route by which a non-steered cell can enter, remain and leave — see [[Microniche]].
