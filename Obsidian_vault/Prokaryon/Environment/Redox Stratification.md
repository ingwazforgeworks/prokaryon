**Concept:** How electron-acceptor availability layers the world  
**Status:** Proposed — depends on specification §7.1's open question about redox complexity

Not a field of its own, but the pattern that [[Oxidex]], [[Sulfex]] and [[Flow]] produce together. It is the single most important piece of geography in the game, because it decides how much [[ATP]] a cell gets from identical food.

## The gradient

[[Oxidex]] is consumed locally and replenished only by transport, so its concentration follows enclosure and current rather than depth alone.

| Zone | Made by | Best acceptor | Yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open flowing water | Strong [[Flow]], surface exchange | [[Oxidex]] | Highest |
| Sheltered water, cave mouths | Reduced flow | Marginal — either, poorly | Transitional |
| Cave interiors, sediment, biofilm centres | Enclosure and consumption | [[Sulfex]] | Moderate |
| Anywhere depleted of both | Crowding | None — fermentation | Lowest, excretes [[Fermentate]] |

## Why it matters more than food

Two cells eating the same [[Glycon]] can differ severalfold in [[ATP]] income depending only on where they are. That makes position a metabolic decision rather than a cosmetic one, and it is why [[Metabolic Map]] lists the aerobic specialist and the sediment chemotroph as separate viable strategies instead of one being strictly better.

The cost of the rich zone is exposure: lit, open, flowing water is also where predators find you, where [[Lysin]] spreads fastest, where oxidative damage demands [[Oxidex Detoxase (OXDT)]], and where every other player wants to be.

## Players deepen the gradient

Stratification is not purely authored. A thickening [[Matrixin]] biofilm impedes transport, so its interior drifts toward low [[Oxidex]] and accumulating [[Fermentate]] — generating a new anoxic niche inside a colony that started in open water. Specification §7.4 asks for at least two outcomes from the same rules under different conditions, and a biofilm growing its own dark centre is one of them.

## The switching problem

A cell drifting between zones cannot afford to express both terminal reductases permanently. [[Redoxin (RDXN)]] exists to make that a regulatory decision, and it reports a single scalar for two acceptors — so transitional water is genuinely ambiguous to the cell. See [[Redoxin (RDXN)]].

## Open

Specification §7.1 asks whether redox stratification is worth the complexity, and offers oxygen alone or a selected proxy as the lightweight option. This vault proposes two acceptors because one acceptor collapses every dark niche into fermentation and removes the reason for the [[Sulfex]] cross-feeding loop. That is a design argument, not a settled decision.
