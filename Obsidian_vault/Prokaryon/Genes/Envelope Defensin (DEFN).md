**Gene ID:** DEFN  
**Gene Name:** Envelope Defensin  
**Category:** [[Homeostasis]]  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Description:** Surface proteins that disrupt contact-dependent attack machinery, preventing an [[Effector Injector (EFFI)]] from completing delivery. Defends against being injected and against nothing else.  
**Inputs:** [[Biomass]], [[ATP]]  
**Outputs:** Reduced probability that an injection attempt succeeds  
**Expression scaling:** [[Saturating Scaling]]  
**Genetic Prerequisites:** None  
**Other Prerequisites:** None  
**Default Localization:** Cell membrane, outward-facing  
**Tradeoff:** The narrowest defence in the catalog, against the rarest attack. It is worthless in a habitat where nobody has bought an injector, and a cell cannot know in advance whether it will meet one.  
**Default counters:** Blocks [[Effector Injector (EFFI)]]; does nothing against [[Lysin]] or [[Bdellase (BDEL)]]  
**Design basis:** Surface modifications and immunity proteins that interfere with contact-dependent delivery.

**It also blocks gifts.** Contact-dependent machinery is how [[Conjugation Apparatus (CONJ)]] transfers genes, so a defensin cell is harder to injure and harder to help. Whether that coupling is exact — one gene blocking both hostile injection and wanted transfer — depends on decisions D19 and D23 in [[Horizontal Gene Transfer]], and on whether specification §8.2's "visible and rejectable" option is chosen over systemic genetic acceptance.

That ambiguity is the interesting part. Defence against interaction is defence against all interaction, and [[Restriction Defense (RSTD)]] has the same shape: there is no configuration that accepts only the good things.
