**Gene ID:** PILN  
**Gene Name:** Pilin  
**Category:** [[Motility]]  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Description:** Builds retractable surface filaments that grip a substrate and pull. The cell crawls rather than swims: slow, short-ranged, and unbothered by current.  
**Inputs:** [[Biomass]], [[ATP]]  
**Outputs:** Surface-bound traction; resistance to [[Flow]]  
**Expression scaling:** [[Linear Expression Scaling]]  
**Genetic Prerequisites:** None  
**Other Prerequisites:** Requires a surface to work against; useless in open water  
**Default Localization:** Cell membrane, polar  
**Tradeoff:** Far slower than a flagellum and confined to surfaces, so a pilus lineage can reposition within a deposit but cannot cross open water to reach a better one.  
**Design basis:** Type IV pilus extension and retraction driving twitching motility.

**Why it exists alongside flagella.** Surface fuels — [[Ferron]] from [[Ferracite]], [[Sulfex]] from [[Thionite]] — require contact, and a flagellum is the wrong tool for staying somewhere. PILN gives a sessile lineage limited mobility without forfeiting the attachment its metabolism depends on, and it costs far less [[ATP]] than [[Flagellar Motor Protein (FLGM)]] because it moves only occasionally.

Paired with [[Adhesin (ADHN)]] it produces the surface-crawling strategy in [[Metabolic Map]]: a lineage that holds a mineral deposit against the current and shuffles across it as patches deplete.
