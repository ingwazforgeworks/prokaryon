**Gene ID:** BFP  
**Gene Name:** Blue Fluorescent Protein  
**Category:** [[Metabolism]]  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Description:** Emits blue light from chemical energy. The glow is the cell's own, and it is independent of the body colour its pigments give it.  
**Inputs:** [[Nitrox]], a little [[Glycon]], [[ATP]]  
**Outputs:** Blue [[Light]], local and weak  
**Expression scaling:** [[Linear Expression Scaling]] — brightness and the ATP drain both follow abundance  
**Genetic Prerequisites:** None  
**Other Prerequisites:** None  
**Default Localization:** [[Cytosol]]  
**Tradeoff:** A blue photon costs more [[ATP]] than a red one, so this is the dearest of the four for the same brightness. The band is the one [[Chlorin]] and [[Carotin]] both take, which is also the end of the spectrum [[Photoprotectin (PHPR)]] exists to survive. A blue cell pays the most to be seen by the lineages already equipped for bright light.  
**Design basis:** Blue fluorescent protein, a spectral variant of green fluorescent protein, collapsed here into one module that spends [[ATP]] to emit.

**A glow, not a pigment.** [[Rhodin]], [[Chlorin]], [[Carotin]] and [[Phycin]] colour the body by absorbing [[Light]]. BFP adds blue light to the water. The two can be carried together, and neither replaces the other. See [[Phototrophy]].
