**Gene ID:** GFP  
**Gene Name:** Green Fluorescent Protein  
**Category:** [[Metabolism]]  
**Status:** Proposed  
**Description:** Emits green light from chemical energy. The glow is the cell's own, and it is independent of the body colour its pigments give it.  
**Inputs:** [[Nitrox]], a little [[Glycon]], [[ATP]]  
**Outputs:** Green [[Light]], local and weak  
**Expression scaling:** [[Linear Expression Scaling]] — brightness and the ATP drain both follow abundance  
**Genetic Prerequisites:** None  
**Other Prerequisites:** None  
**Default Localization:** [[Cytosol]]  
**Tradeoff:** Green is the light a [[Chlorin]] canopy leaves behind, which is exactly what [[Phycin]] harvests. Under that canopy the glow blends into water that is already green, so the cell spends [[ATP]] to look like the shade. In the dark, or in open water, the same colour is a beacon, and every Phycin neighbour can eat it.  
**Design basis:** Green fluorescent protein from *Aequorea*. In the jellyfish the protein is the colour converter coupled to a bioluminescent reaction; here the converter and the emission are one gene.

**A glow, not a pigment.** [[Rhodin]], [[Chlorin]], [[Carotin]] and [[Phycin]] colour the body by absorbing [[Light]]. GFP adds green light to the water. The two can be carried together, and neither replaces the other. See [[Phototrophy]].
