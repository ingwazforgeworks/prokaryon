**Region Name:** Nuclear Membrane  
**Status:** Proposed — depends on the unresolved scope conflict recorded in [[Nucleus]]  
**Available from:** The eukaryotic transition only  
**Separates:** [[Nucleus]] from [[Cytosol]]

The boundary that makes a [[Nucleus]] mean anything. Without a barrier, a nuclear compartment is just a labelled region of cytosol.

### It is a gate, not a wall

The membrane admits proteins carrying a [[NuclearLocalizationSignal]] and excludes those that do not. Everything interesting about eukaryotic regulation in this design follows from that one rule:

- A regulator without the tag is produced and never used, which is a legible and instructive failure
- A regulator with the tag arrives late, so regulation has latency the player can reason about
- Metabolic enzymes stay out by default, so the genome is no longer sitting in the middle of the cell's chemistry

### A second localization surface

Like the [[Plasma Membrane]], this is a membrane with an inside and an outside, so it can host anchored proteins — which means the eukaryotic cell has two distinct membranes to target rather than one.

Whether the design should use that is a real question. It doubles the localization space for a payoff that is not yet identified, and unused options are worse than absent ones. The conservative choice is to keep the nuclear membrane as a gate only, and add anchoring to it later if a gene actually needs it.

### Open question — does it cost anything to maintain?

A membrane is material and upkeep. If it is free, the nucleus is a pure gain and the concern raised in [[Nucleus]] about prokaryotes becoming obsolete gets worse. A standing [[Biomass]] and [[ATP]] cost for maintaining the envelope is the simplest honest answer, and it gives the eukaryotic form a genuine disadvantage in poor water.
